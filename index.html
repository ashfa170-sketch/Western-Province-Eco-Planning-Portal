Methods & transparency

What each layer means

Environmental layers

Wetlands & marshes: mapped wetland-related features in the supplied dataset.

Mangroves: mapped mangrove features in the supplied dataset.

Lagoons & waterbodies: mapped water features, including lagoon-related records.

100 m coastal planning buffer: a project-defined spatial reference layer; it should not be interpreted as a universal statutory setback.

Development layers

500 m building-pressure cells: building footprints near mapped sensitive areas were aggregated into 500 m cells by development category. This keeps the browser fast and highlights concentration rather than individual buildings.

Land-use change: the supplied trajectory records classified land use for 2005, 2015 and 2025. The slider shows the selected year.

Potential encroachment

Potential encroachment sites: supplied spatial evidence of development pressure/encroachment. The label is deliberately cautious: spatial evidence alone does not establish that development is illegal or unauthorized.

Planning priority: an analytical screening layer based on the concentration of development pressure in the supplied 500 m cells. It is a prioritisation aid, not a statutory zoning category.

Public participation

People can select a location and submit an anonymous observation through the linked Google Form. Submitted coordinates are read from the published sheet and displayed on the map. Reports are marked unverified until reviewed.

Important: project analytical distances and classifications are working assumptions for the web map unless a separate authoritative planning or environmental regulation is cited. They should be checked against official Sri Lankan regulations and plans in the final report.
